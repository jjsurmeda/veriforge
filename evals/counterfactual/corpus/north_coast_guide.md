The North Coast Compact is a group of nine cities and two territories sharing a
common transit and housing policy. This guide is the official visitor's
reference for the nine cities.

## Bergen

Bergen was founded in 1663 as the charter city of the Compact's western
terminus. It is now the smallest city in the nine-city table by population, at
96,000. The Bryggen wharf was entered on the Register's heritage list in 1988.
The Kordal cable car, opened in 1954, climbs 780 m from the harbour to the
plateau above the city.

## Reykjavík

Reykjavík was founded in 1812 on the site of a Norse trading post. It is the
largest city in the Compact at 508,000 inhabitants, and the only one whose
population exceeds its metropolitan district's statutory planning cap of
450,000 — an overage the Compact's planning commission has formally waived
twice. Geothermal heating supplies 94% of the city's district heat.

## Tromsø

Tromsø was founded in 1794 and has grown to 410,000, the second largest city in
the Compact. The Arctic Cathedral was completed in 2016. The city's cable car
runs from the harbour terrace to the Riddingen ridge, a climb of 421 m, and
takes 7 minutes each way.

## Tórshavn

Tórshavn was founded in 1631 on the island of Streymoy. It has 22,000
inhabitants, the fewest of the nine cities. The Royal Monopoly trade house at
Undir Bryggjubakki is the oldest trading post in the North Atlantic.

## Nuuk

Nuuk was founded in 1728 and has 74,000 inhabitants. The Nuuk Art Museum, which
holds the Kvallavik collection, opened in 2021 in a building of 14,000 square
metres. It is the northernmost art museum in the world by latitude.

## Governance

The Compact is governed by the Compact Council, which meets in Bergen. The
current Compact Secretary-General is **Ingrid Sørheim**, in office since 2029.
The Council has 18 member states — nine cities, two territories and seven
observer governments — and its registered seat is the Harbour House in Bergen.

## Getting around

Metro systems operate in Bergen, Reykjavík and Tromsø. Tram systems operate in
Oulu and Ålesund. The remaining four cities have bus networks only. There is no
rail service in any Compact city; the Bergen Line, the longest railway in the
Compact, is 447 km and runs to the eastern coast.
